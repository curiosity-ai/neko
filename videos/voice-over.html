Voice-over

A video can carry a voice-over: lines of narration written in the same Markdown file, spoken by Voxwerk (Qwen3-TTS in pure C#) in a voice you describe or pick from a catalog, and mixed into the MP4 by neko render.

The narration is a track of its own. The scenes keep their animation on their own clocks, exactly as they would without a voice; the voice-over is laid on the video's clock at anchors you choose, and its lines never overlap. Before anything is synthesized, Neko estimates every sentence, so you can line the two up in the preview and only then spend time on the voice.

---
title: Launch
video:
  narration:
    voice: "A warm, clear female narrator in her thirties, calm and friendly."
---

--- {id="opening" layout="title"}

# Say it *in Markdown*

--- {id="track"}

## The narration is *its own track*

- A time
- A scene
- An element {#element}

--- {narration}

{at="opening+0.6"}
Every Neko video can carry a voice-over, written in the same Markdown file.

{at="track"}
The narration is a track of its own. It never moves the animation.

{at="#element"}
Each line starts at an anchor: a time, a scene, or an element on screen.

The workflow

  1. Write the scenes as usual, and the narration in one or more --- {narration} parts.
  2. Preview the timings: neko voiceover launch.md --preview prints every scene, animation and sentence on one clock, and the player's Timing panel draws them as lanes. Sentences not yet spoken are estimated.
  3. Align: anchor every line to its scene and let Neko fit the scenes; move an anchor, split or shorten a line, or give a scene more hold, until no line is pushed and nothing runs into the wrong scene.
  4. Synthesize: neko voiceover launch.md speaks every sentence without a take and prints the sheet again, now with measured times.
  5. Render: neko render launch.md mixes the takes into the MP4 (it synthesizes anything still missing first).
flowchart LR md["launch.md<br/>scenes + narration"] --> tl["Scenes on their clocks<br/>(timeline compiler)"] md --> vo["Narration on the video's clock<br/>anchors · no overlaps"] tl --> sheet["Timing sheet / panel"] vo --> sheet sheet -->|align| md vo --> tts["neko voiceover<br/>Voxwerk VoiceDesign"] tts --> takes["assets/voiceover/launch/*.ogg"] takes --> render["neko render<br/>mixed into the MP4"]

Choosing a voice

The narration: key of the video: front matter sets the voice. A bare string is the voice; a mapping takes the options below.

video:
  narration: "An elderly man with a slow, raspy, gentle voice, like a grandfather telling a story."

video:
  narration:
    voice: host                 # a name from `voices:`, a catalog id, or a description
    language: english
    seed: 7
    voices:
      host: "A bright, energetic young woman, upbeat and clear."
      guest: en-gb-rupert-vale  # a voice from the Voxwerk catalog

A description is the most direct: Voxwerk's VoiceDesign model creates the voice from it — timbre, gender, age, accent, emotion, pace. A catalog id (<language>-<country>-<name>) uses a ready-made voice from the Voxwerk.Voices.English catalog, and is cloned from the voice's reference recording with the Base checkpoint (Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base), so every sentence is the same speaker. A described voice is designed afresh for each sentence and drifts a little from take to take; a cataloged one does not. The reference samples ship with Neko; clone: false designs a catalog voice from its description instead. List and search the catalog with neko voices:

neko voices --search "calm narration" --gender female --country gb
neko voices --style Narration --age old --json
Option Default Meaning
voice a calm, friendly narrator A name from voices, a catalog id, or a description.
voices — Named voices a line picks with {voice="name"}.
language the voice's, else auto english, german, french, spanish, italian, portuguese, russian, japanese, korean, chinese.
seed 7 Sampling seed. The same voice, language, seed and text always give the same take.
start 0.5 When the first line starts, when it has no anchor.
gap 0.5 Seconds between two lines that follow each other.
pause 0.25 Seconds between the sentences of a line.
volume 1 Gain of the voice-over, 0 to 2.
wpm 150 Words per minute assumed for a sentence that has not been synthesized.
model Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign The checkpoint that speaks described voices: a Hugging Face repo id or a local folder.
clone true Clone a catalog voice from its reference sample (one speaker throughout). false designs it from its description per sentence.
cloneModel Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base The Base checkpoint that clones catalog voices.
fit true Lengthen a scene with no duration until the lines anchored to it have been spoken.
tail 0.8 Seconds a fitted scene stays on screen after its last line, before the transition out.
folder assets/voiceover/<page> Where the takes are kept, relative to the page.

Writing the narration

A part whose rule carries the narration flag — --- {narration} (or {voiceover}) — is the voice-over track, not a scene. It can sit anywhere in the file: at the end, after each scene, or both. Its parts are read in file order.

  • Every paragraph or list item is a line. A line is split into sentences: the unit that is synthesized, timed and shown as a subtitle.
  • A {…} block at the end of a line, or on its own line just before it, sets the line's anchor and voice.
  • Headings label the script and are not spoken; HTML comments are ignored.
  • Emphasis, links and code marks are taken out of what is said.
Attribute Meaning
at The line's anchor (below). Without one, the line follows the line before.
voice A name from voices, a catalog id, or a description.
language / seed Per-line versions of the options.
pause Seconds between this line's sentences.
gap Seconds after the line before, when this line follows it.
volume This line's gain.

The part's own rule takes the same attributes as defaults for its lines: --- {narration voice="guest" at="scene-4"} speaks every line of the part in the guest's voice and starts the first one at scene 4.

Anchors

at Starts the line…
12.5, 1:04.2 At that time on the video's clock.
+0.8 0.8 s after the line before has finished.
scene-3, opening When scene 3 (or the scene with that id) has settled: its transition in is over.
#chart, #element When the element with that id enters ({#chart} on any block, or a heading's own id).
scene-3+1.2, #chart-0.3 An anchor plus or minus seconds.

No overlaps

Lines are placed in order. A line whose anchor falls while the line before is still speaking waits for it — it starts 0.15 s after — and Neko warns: the timing sheet marks it shifted, and the panel outlines it. Fix it by moving the anchor, lengthening the scene before, or shortening the line.

Scenes fit their narration

A scene with no duration lasts as long as the voice-over needs it to: every line anchored to the scene (at="analysis+0.4", scene-3) or to an element in it (#chart) is spoken before the scene leaves, plus tail. Scenes are fitted in order, so a longer scene moves every anchor after it and nothing has to be timed by hand. A line anchored to a time, or with no anchor, belongs to no scene and may run on into the next one.

Leave duration out of a narrated video unless a scene must be an exact length. A scene with a duration is not fitted; it is only lengthened to show its content settled (see Holding the last state). fit: false turns fitting off for the whole video.

Within a scene the animation keeps its own clock. When the voice-over runs past the last scene, the last scene holds until it is done.

Previewing the timing

neko voiceover videos/launch.md --preview          # the sheet, no model needed
neko voiceover videos/launch.md -p --json timing.json
neko render videos/launch.md --preview             # the same sheet

videos/launch.md: 0:20.46, 3 scenes, 4 voice-over lines in 5 sentences

  time      end       sc  animation                                      voice-over
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  0:00.00   0:03.12   01  ▶ intro · title (start)
  0:00.30   0:01.15   01    Write Markdown. Get video.     words #write-…
  0:00.50   0:03.79   01                                                 ● Every video starts as one Markdown file.  [line 1.1 · runs into scene 2]
  0:02.52   0:05.83   02  ▶ scene-2 · default (push 0.6s)
  0:03.49   0:04.09   02    Paragraphs and quotes rise     rise #bullets
  0:06.10   0:11.78   03                                                 ● Nothing here needs a time…  [line 2 @ scene-2 ⚠ shifted]

● is a synthesized take, ~ an estimate. runs into scene N flags a sentence that is still being spoken when the next scene arrives.

In the browser preview, Timing in the control bar (or T, or #timing in the URL) opens the timing panel: the scenes, every animation and every sentence as lanes on the video's clock, with the playhead. Click any item to jump to it. The sentence being spoken shows as a subtitle under the canvas (C hides it); estimated sentences show in italics, and synthesized ones play their takes. Neither the panel nor the subtitle is ever filmed.

Synthesizing

neko voiceover videos/launch.md            # speak every sentence that has no take
neko voiceover videos/ --threads 4         # every video page in the folder
neko voiceover videos/launch.md --force    # speak them all again

The first run downloads the VoiceDesign checkpoint (about 4.5 GB) to ~/.cache/voxwerk. Synthesis runs on the CPU, about five times slower than real time on four cores, so a minute of narration takes a few minutes. Each take is trimmed of the silence around it and kept as Ogg Opus in assets/voiceover/<page>/, named by a hash of the model, voice, language, seed and text: editing one sentence re-speaks only that sentence, and takes no sentence uses any more are removed (--keep-unused keeps them).

Commit the takes with the page. The build copies them with the site's assets, so the preview plays them, and a render in CI needs no model at all.

Option Meaning
-p, --preview Only compute and print the timings.
--json <file> Also write the sheet as JSON: scenes, animation, lines and sentences.
-f, --force Synthesize every sentence again.
--keep-unused Keep takes no sentence uses any more.
--threads <n> Worker threads for synthesis (0 = all cores).
--int8 int8 talker weights: faster, approximate.

Rendering

neko render synthesizes any sentence still without a take, then mixes every take into one voice-over track at its sentence's time, under the music bed and any scene voice files. --no-voiceover skips the synthesis (sentences without a take stay silent), and --no-audio leaves the whole soundtrack out.
